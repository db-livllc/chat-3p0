# Spark

Open creative requests pull toward the most typical answer: the one most people, and most models, would give. Told only to "be original", a model swaps one default for another and lands in the same quirky place every time. So this skill takes part of the choice out of your hands.

## 1. Roll

Before you think about the request, get three random numbers from 1 to 20 from the computer. Do not pick them yourself: a model's "random" picks are the same ones every time.

- PowerShell: `1..3 | ForEach-Object { Get-Random -Minimum 1 -Maximum 21 }`
- bash or zsh: `for i in 1 2 3; do echo $((RANDOM % 20 + 1)); done`

If you cannot run commands, use the current time if you can see it: the minute for the first number, the second for the next, their sum for the third, wrapping past 20. Failing that, take from each list an entry you would not normally choose for this request.

Use the numbers in order: a voice, a way in, and a letter.

**Voice**
1 deadpan · 2 spare and austere · 3 menacing · 4 tender · 5 bureaucratic · 6 mythic · 7 clinical · 8 gossipy · 9 elegiac · 10 exuberant · 11 hard-boiled · 12 pastoral · 13 absurd played straight · 14 documentary · 15 devotional · 16 satirical · 17 childlike · 18 instructional · 19 confessional · 20 ceremonial

**Way in**
1 through a minor figure nobody watches · 2 through a document (a receipt, a manual, meeting minutes) · 3 starting at the end · 4 through a sound · 5 through a rule and its one exception · 6 at the wrong scale, far too big or too small · 7 through a job nobody mentions · 8 through an earlier failed attempt · 9 through a ritual · 10 through a trade or a debt · 11 through a rumour · 12 through a map or a route · 13 through a meal · 14 through weather · 15 through a repair · 16 through a game and its rules · 17 through a border or a threshold · 18 through an inheritance · 19 through a mistake that stuck · 20 through what is missing

**Letter**
1 A · 2 B · 3 C · 4 D · 5 E · 6 F · 7 G · 8 H · 9 I · 10 J · 11 K · 12 L · 13 M · 14 N · 15 O · 16 P · 17 R · 18 S · 19 T · 20 W

The roll is a constraint to design within. The voice sets how the piece sounds. The way in sets where it enters the subject. The letter begins the name or title; if the piece has neither, it begins the first thing the piece names. The lists are prompts, not vocabulary: keep their own words, such as "ritual", "rumour" or "ceremonial", out of the piece. If one roll truly cannot serve the request, roll that number again once.

## 2. Find the direction

Privately:

1. Name the three answers most likely to be given for this request, including the first name, setting and motif that come to mind. Those are off limits, along with small variations of them.
2. Name two habits of your own for this kind of piece: the figure you tend to put at the centre (a parent, a child narrator), the era or place you drift to, a number or image you reuse. Those are off limits too.
3. Sketch four different directions that satisfy both the request and the roll. Estimate how likely a typical response would be to go each way. Keep only directions under 10%.
4. Pick the one that best serves what the user asked for.

## 3. Make it

Make the piece well. Specific beats general: concrete nouns, exact details, a name that could only belong to this piece. Deliver the finished piece only, at the length the request implies. Don't show the roll, the alternatives or the method.

Avoid stock phrasing: "delve", "tapestry", "whisper", "echo", "a testament to", "nestled", "ember", "luminous", and the "isn't X, it's Y" pattern. Avoid generic names built from one evocative noun, such as Haven, Ember, Echo, Nexus or Bloom.
